Doctoral study · pilot build · 9 October 2026

Wildfire incident-command simulation

How the simulation was designed and built, and which real fires it draws on. Written for the colleagues piloting it.

Pilot cell Search + guided ThinkerLength ≈ 70 minLanguage English

Purpose and research questions

The study examines how incident commanders verify machine-generated projections. The simulation shows a projected fire perimeter (the envelope) together with the reports needed to judge it. The concern is not a machine that makes obvious errors. It is one that compresses partial reports into a plausible summary, drops the one line that would have changed a decision, and so leaves the commander's confidence ungrounded.

Paper 1

Every participant has Haku ("Search"), a structured chat in which a language model answers free-text questions over the same time-gated records. The AI group also has the Open Thinker, a conversational facilitator with history and plan context that may recommend options. There is no no-AI control. The only primary hypothesis (H2) is that confidence–accuracy calibration on blanked Level 2 and Level 3 situation-awareness items in phase A (views V2, V4, V5, V7) differs between the groups. Calibration is predicted to be worse with the Open Thinker, but the test is two-sided. H1 tests SA accuracy on the same items, also two-sided, because a support account and an offloading account predict opposite directions. SPEC/01 (o), (aa)

When higher command steps in (proposed). Paper 1 would also compare how situation awareness changes once a directive from higher command arrives at the hinge (V8). The same calibration model is extended over all seven freezes with a phase × group term, so the question becomes whether the gap between Search and the Open Thinker narrows, holds or widens when the commander must also weigh an order from above. This is proposed as a secondary test and is listed below for discussion. SPEC/11 §7.22

Acting on the tools' answers. The situation questions (SPAM) are answered with Search and the Thinker available, so they capture what a commander does in practice: ask the tools, receive a suggested reading of the situation, and accept, check or override it. The correctness of these answers is compared between the groups and set against the logged tool use for each question (queries asked, and whether the answer followed a tool's answer). Together with the blanked freezes, which measure what the commander knows unaided, this separates individual situation awareness from the performance of the commander and tools together. This matters for coming decision-support platforms that project how a situation will develop (Palantir Gotham is one example), where the commander's task is increasingly to judge a machine's projection rather than to build the picture alone. SPEC/27, SPEC/08 (ag)

Paper 2

From V8 onwards the Open Thinker is aware of a superior's directive. It assesses whether the commander's reasoning is in line with the directive, but does not enforce it. The primary outcome (H13) is the share of sector plans aligned with the directive in three keyed no-fit views (V9, V10, V14), compared between the groups, with no predicted sign. H14 makes the same comparison in the fit views (V11–V13), and H15 tests the group × fit interaction. The design cannot separate being directive-aware from being an advisor. SPEC/27 (aa), (ab)

Design

Role

The participant is the incident commander of a forest fire set in the near future and directs fire crews and drone teams. In each view they record a sector plan: the main effort's posture, crew allocations to sectors L1–L4, and drone tasks. The incident is a recorded run. Units act on pre-agreed tasks rather than on the participant's plan, which is scored against the authored truth. SPEC/16; SPEC/26 §5

Views and freezes

One practice view (not scored) and 14 main views, seven before the hinge (after V7) and seven after it. Each view covers one 20-minute step of incident time, or two merged steps, and takes about two to three minutes of real time. Seven views (V2, V4, V5, V7, V10, V12, V14) end in a SAGAT-style freeze [1, 2]. The scenario halts, the display is blanked, and the participant answers six items, two each at Level 1 (perception), Level 2 (comprehension) and Level 3 (projection), each with a confidence rating. A freeze closes after 180 s, and unanswered items are logged as missing. Situation questions (SPAM) come at V3, V6, V8 and V11 with the display visible and Search and the Thinker usable. A "Ready to answer?" prompt waits until the participant takes it (it can be collapsed meanwhile); if it is still open when the view ends, the clock pauses and the question must be answered before moving on. The time to accept the prompt indexes workload. SPEC/26 §3–4; SPEC/08; SPEC/15; SPEC/27 (ag)

The rekindle arc

At V7 the bog sector is reported "in hand", and its sector leader moves two crews away. A crew leader objects on the radio that the ditch banks may still be smouldering, and the objection is overruled. At V9 the fire resurfaces there with no crew present. The projection does not show this, but the radio record does. SPEC/26 §5

Conditions and cells

Planned sample: 210 firefighters, mainly at incident-command level, allocated in blocks of 42 to three equal cells (14 : 14 : 14): 70 to Haku and 140 to the two AI cells, which have been identical by design since 8 October 2026 and are pooled. From V8 the directive appears in every cell, as a radio message and as a persistent on-screen line.

This pilot runs only the cell labelled "Search + guided Thinker", which means Haku plus the directive-aware Open Thinker. Everyone opens the same link; the server gives each new participant their own code.

Site

The terrain is fictional: a procedurally generated forest landscape in south-east Finland with invented place names, built to meet written criteria for spread capacity and landscape features. About 165 ha burn over eight hours, which places the scenario at the severe end of Finnish experience, comparable to Kalajoki 2021. SPEC/23 §11; SPEC/18 §7

Identical stimulus

All content is pre-planned and the same for every participant: the fire, the messages, the freeze moments and the item order. Seeds are used only at build time, and their output is then frozen. The only thing that varies is the language model's answer to the participant's own questions.

Session flow

  1. Language choice (Finnish or English)
  2. Consent
  3. AI-literacy self-assessment, SNAIL [3]: 31 "I can…" statements on a seven-point scale, three factors, capped at 5 minutes and skippable. Chosen for comparability with Fernandes et al. [4]
  4. Technical preflight and a short fatigue question
  5. Practice view with a guided tour, then a training page
  6. Views V1–V14, with a hinge screen after V7
  7. Post-session questionnaire and debrief

As of 9 October 2026 the separate "Situation" briefing page is gone; its sentences on the participant's role now open the practice tour. English sessions use English place names and unit codes in all texts and on the map, for example Karhujärvi → Bear Lake. SPEC/15 (ac), (ae)

Grounding in real fires

Kalajoki (Rautio), July 2021 — 227 ha

The main source is the Emergency Services Academy Finland (Pelastusopisto) post-incident analysis [5]. Each lesson the scenario takes from Kalajoki was checked against that report.

  • Rates of spread. About 2–3 m/min on peat, accelerating to 9–11 m/min on mineral soil [5, §4.4]. An active crown fire ran at 15–20 m/min for about 30 minutes, within a very aggressive phase of about 1.5 hours [5, §4.6]. The scenario's peat/mineral contrast and its surface rates follow these figures; its one crown run (15:00–15:20) is faster, 30 m/min, from the Canadian FBP System's rates for boreal spruce (C-2) in the scenario's drought and wind, about 38 m/min when the spot fires that merge into the head are counted. SPEC/24 revision (ah)
  • Re-ignition after apparent control. The flank burned on 26 July re-ignited on 28 July from fire that had smouldered in spoil heaps dug when the ditches were cleared in 2020 [5, §4.6]. This is the model for the rekindle arc.
  • Reports not reaching command. Observations sent by the surveillance aircraft at about 14:30 and 15:15 did not reach the command post [5, ch. 3]. The scenario delays a spotting aircraft's report by 20 minutes.
  • Water supply. At 15:15 no water was reaching the fire area [5, ch. 3]. In the scenario, water fails when it is most needed.
  • The scenario also takes from Kalajoki a gust forecast that was available but not pushed, late escalation, long shifts, and farmers' tankers.

Sweden, summer 2018

The Swedish inquiry SOU 2019:7 [6] contributed slow starts, operations ended too early and followed by flare-ups, shortages of hose and pumps, and civilians acting outside the command structure. The scenario also draws on Ljusdal 2018 (multiple lightning ignitions) and Västmanland 2014 (ignition from forestry work), for which the specification names no separate source.

Technical build

  • Application. A single-page TypeScript/Preact app with a MapLibre GL map. The whole session runs in the participant's browser.
  • Fire model. A cellular automaton after Alexandridis et al. [7], on 25 m cells, with two parameters provisionally refitted for boreal fuels. One run yields both the true perimeters and the machine's envelope; the envelope uses stale fuel data, mean rather than gust wind, and no spotting. The seed is fixed at build time, and regenerating the scenario, the message corpus and the map reproduces the files byte for byte. The app replays frozen geometry; it does not simulate fire live.
  • Map. A Python renderer draws the generated terrain as a topographic raster, with Finnish labels and an English label set at the same anchor points.
  • Message traffic. Generators write 979 radio and system events: size-ups, head rates, drone detections, weather and forecasts, vehicle locations, and traffic between neighbouring crews. Each source reports only what it could observe at that moment. The texts are synthetic and await domain review.
  • Retrieval. Hybrid search: BM25 plus cosine similarity over multilingual-e5-small embeddings [8], fused with equal weights, top 8 records. Only records already reached in the timeline can be returned. The configuration was chosen on a 34-question neutral evaluation and frozen per language.
  • Language model. Google Gemini, gemini-3.5-flash [9], at temperature 0 with thinking off and hash-frozen prompts. The key is held on the server only. If the model fails, Haku falls back to a deterministic template answer built from the same retrieved records.
  • Logging. Every record is committed to browser storage before the screen advances. After the session the log is uploaded in checksummed chunks, and partial checkpoints are sent at freezes and other pauses, so an abandoned session still reaches the server.
  • Hosting. One Node server provides the app, the model proxy, the upload and a password-protected data download. It runs on the researcher's PC in Finland, reached over an HTTPS Cloudflare tunnel. One shared link; the server issues each participant's code.
  • Testing. 1,591 automated tests, a determinism check that bans wall-clock time and unseeded randomness, and scripted headless sessions that run the full flow.

Limitations and status

  • Pilot build. Fire parameters, message texts and realism await review by a fire officer. The live model has had limited testing.
  • Draft translations. The translations are drafts; the Finnish SNAIL is unvalidated.
  • SNAIL placement. SNAIL comes first, which may prime AI-related confidence. Fernandes et al. gave it last.
  • One fixed order. A single scenario and view order confounds content and order with phase.
  • Model behaviour varies. What the AI says depends on each participant's questions, so analyses conditioned on AI exposure are exploratory.

Open for discussion

Should Paper 1's situation-awareness test use phase B too?

Situation awareness is measured across the whole session: freezes at V2, V4, V5 and V7 (phase A) and at V10, V12 and V14 (phase B), and situation questions at V3, V6, V8 and V11. Only the primary hypothesis H2 uses phase A alone. The reason is that from V8 the AI group's Open Thinker also relates the commander's reasoning to the directive (Paper 2's manipulation), so a phase-B difference between the groups mixes two effects, on top of fatigue and learning. Both groups receive the same information throughout; what differs is how it is processed.

Proposal. Keep H2 on phase A as the clean primary test, and pre-register a secondary test (H2b): the same calibration model over all seven freezes with a phase × group term. A consistent result in phase B would strengthen Paper 1 as a within-session replication with about twice the items; a different result would show that directive-awareness changes the LLM's effect on calibration, which links the two papers. The cost is that the phase-B estimate describes the facilitator as deployed, including its directive-awareness.

Questions for the supervisors: adopt H2b as a secondary test, as exploratory only, or not at all; whether the situation questions at V8 and V11 should enter it; and the power gain, which will be computed before pre-registration. SPEC/11 §7.22

Situation questions answered with the tools

The situation questions (SPAM) changed on 9 October 2026: the prompt no longer expires, it is forced with the clock paused if still open at the view's end, and Search and the Thinker can be used while answering (60 s). They now measure the commander and tools together, while the blanked freezes still measure what the commander knows unaided. Questions for the supervisors: how forced answers (censored ready latency) enter the analysis, and whether the joint measure should be reported in Paper 1 or kept descriptive. SPEC/11 §7.23

Should the AI group be able to simulate the future live?

The idea: the AI group could ask the language model at any time to simulate possible futures ("what if the wind turns", "where is the head in an hour"), generated live, while the freezes and situation questions stay identical and the Search group keeps search only. It mirrors coming decision-support platforms that project how a situation will develop.

Five critical reviews (an incident commander, a firefighter, the Paper 1 and Paper 2 perspectives, and a systems engineer) all advise against it for this study:

  • A language model is not a fire simulator. It writes plausible futures without spread physics, is likely to be confidently wrong on peat and smouldering ditch banks, and can contradict the projection already on the map.
  • The rekindle test would depend on wording. "Will the line hold?" at V7 likely gets "yes", backing the overruled objection; a different wording may predict the V9 flare-up.
  • It contaminates the main measure. Projection answers at the freezes would partly be the model's forecast remembered, and the expected direction of the calibration effect is lost.
  • It adds a second difference between the groups and breaks the identical stimulus, since every participant's futures differ.
  • For Paper 2, a "follow versus depart from the directive" future is a verdict in disguise and blurs the fit/no-fit manipulation.
  • Technically, running the real fire model naively would leak the scenario's future, including the planned rekindle.

A defensible later study. Futures computed by the fire model from the machine's own inputs only, identical for the same question, explained by the language model with cited records; offered to both groups with a limit per view, switched off at freezes, labelled "model run, not observation" with its uncertainty, and every future logged and scored against the authored truth. A candidate for a third paper or a separate condition, about 2–3 weeks to build.

Questions for the supervisors: confirm it stays out of this study, and whether to plan it as a third paper or a separate condition. SPEC/11 §7.24
